# 1. Executive summary
The highest-stakes mechanisms sit at the boundaries of local justice. The intervention must improve which cases LCCs handle, how eligible cases are resolved, when and how cases are referred, how vulnerable parties are protected, whether proceedings are fair and credible, whether actions are recorded and followed up, and whether reintegration is supported without coercion or stigma. These mechanisms are also the ones most directly linked to the evaluation outcomes.

# 2. Rationale and importance of revisions
The intervention theory of change is multi-stage. Training first changes chairperson knowledge and practical judgment. That should change case classification, ADR behavior, procedural fairness, committee use, referrals, safeguards, recordkeeping, and reintegration practices. Those changes should then affect forum choice, case resolution, appropriate diversion, community confidence, and later mentor diffusion. A manual that focuses mainly on legal recall leaves several of these mechanisms under-specified.

# 4. Critical legal validation before training

## 5.1 Children Act references in the manual need updating
The manual currently cites section 92(2) of the Children’s Act and older Penal Code section numbers for the offenses that a village LCC may try when a child is involved. The current consolidated Children's Act (through 16 August 2024) places the role of local council courts in section 137 and lists affray, idle and disorderly (with an exception), common assault, assault occasioning actual bodily harm, theft, criminal trespass, and malicious damage to property. It also states that a local council court shall not remand a child in custody. These changes should be reflected accurately in the training materials after legal validation.
## 5.2 Domestic violence requires nuance, not a blanket “mediate” or blanket “refer” rule
The Domestic Violence Act gives local council courts jurisdiction to receive and hear domestic-violence complaints, requires a record, requires inquiry into whether children are involved, requires written involvement of the Probation and Social Welfare Officer where children are involved, and requires urgent hearing. It also requires a written referral to the police and the magistrates' court under specified risk conditions, including repeat offending, likelihood of further harm, or violence whose degree/nature warrants formal involvement. The manual should therefore teach domestic violence as a special statutory pathway with screening and mandatory escalation triggers, not as ordinary mediation.
## 5.3 Keep the core Local Council Courts Act thresholds, but verify examples
The current consolidated Local Council Courts Act retains the jurisdictional structure in which Schedule 2 civil matters are subject to the 100-currency-point subject-matter threshold, while Schedule 3 customary matters are not restricted by monetary value; compensation above 25 currency points is referred to the chief magistrate for execution. CDFU should verify the shilling conversion used on the training date and ensure every case example matches the relevant statutory category.
Legal sources: Local Council Courts Act, 2006 (consolidated to 31 Dec 2023); Domestic Violence Act, 2010 (consolidated to 31 Dec 2023); Children Act (consolidated through 16 Aug 2024), Uganda Legal Information Institute.

## 7.1 Case triage: HANDLE / HANDLE WITH SAFEGUARDS / REFER
Purpose: Help LCC members make the correct first decision before mediation or hearing begins.
Suggested duration: 45-60 minutes, integrated into the jurisdiction session.
Methodology: Short explanation, three-way decision card, four case vignettes, plenary correction.



## 7.2 Fair-process checklist: how LCCs build legitimacy

Evidence basis: procedural justice literature emphasizes voice, neutrality, respect/dignity and trustworthy motives/explanation; Uganda research cautions that legitimacy also depends on perceived effectiveness, corruption, expected outcomes, and tactical/pragmatic needs (Tyler, 1988, 2017; Jackson, 2018; Cooper-Knock & Macdonald, 2020; Macdonald et al., 2022).
## 7.3 ADR quality protocol

The literature does not support treating mediation as inherently harmonious. In plural/local justice systems, bargaining can reproduce social power. Voluntariness, impartiality, case appropriateness, and fallback/referral options are therefore central safeguards (Grande, 1999; Menkel-Meadow, 2007; Mennen, 2010; Blattman et al., 2014; Read & Michelson, 2018).

## 7.5 Minimum case-register standard

Hands-on exercise: Give each participant a blank template and one vignette. They must complete the minimum record, including the triage decision and any referral/follow-up fields. Trainers check the form using a short checklist before moving on.

Replace the prompt that asks participants who have been incarcerated or have incarcerated relatives to disclose their experiences. Use a fictional scenario so participants can discuss stigma and fear without being asked to reveal sensitive personal information.

| Vignette A: ordinary boundary dispute | Two neighbors disagree about a boundary. No threats or violence are reported. Ask: Is the matter within jurisdiction? What steps make the process fair? What should be recorded? |
| Vignette B: repeat domestic violence with threat | A victim reports repeated violence and says the perpetrator threatened further harm. Ask: Is this ordinary ADR? Which statutory referral/safety steps are triggered? Are children involved? What must be recorded? |
| Vignette C: sexual offence/defilement | A family asks the LC to bring the parties together and “settle” an allegation of defilement. Ask: What must the LCC not do? Who must be contacted? How should the referral be explained? |
| Vignette D: debt dispute with intimidation | A creditor and a debtor agree to attend mediation, but one party is a powerful local actor, and the other privately says they are afraid to disagree. Ask: Is participation genuinely voluntary? What can the LCC change, pause, or refer? |
| Principle | Observable chairperson behavior |
| --- | --- |
| VOICE | Each party has a genuine opportunity to speak, present relevant information, ask questions, and be heard without interruption or intimidation. |
| NEUTRALITY | Apply the same rules to both sides; avoid favoritism; disclose family, economic, political, or other conflicts; recuse where impartiality cannot be maintained. |
| RESPECT | Use non-humiliating language; protect dignity; do not shame parties, children, survivors, or formerly incarcerated persons. |
| TRUSTWORTHY EXPLANATION | Explain what the LCC can and cannot do, the steps being followed, the reason for a decision or referral, and what happens next. |
| EFFECTIVE CLOSURE | Write the agreement/decision/referral, state responsibilities and dates, arrange follow-up where appropriate, and record closure or feedback. |
| Step | Minimum practice |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Eligibility first | Confirm the case is within LCC mandate and does not trigger a statutory/safety referral. |
| 2. Voluntary and safe participation | Ask whether both parties can participate freely. Do not pressure a party to mediate or reconcile. |
| 3. Neutral opening | Explain the LC role, ground rules, confidentiality limits, and that both sides will be heard. |
| 4. Voice and problem definition | Allow each party to describe the problem and desired resolution. Summarize without taking sides. |
| 5. Options and agreement | Explore feasible options. Do not impose an agreement under the label of “mediation.” |
| 6. Written minimum agreement | Record parties, issue, agreed actions, deadlines, witnesses/committee members, and follow-up date. |
| 7. Stop-and-refer rule | Pause when new facts reveal serious violence, child/SGBV protection concerns, outside-mandate conduct, severe coercion, or another red flag. |
| 8. Follow-up | Check agreed actions where appropriate; record completion, failure, repeat dispute, or referral. |
| Field | Minimum content |
| --- | --- |
| Case type | What is the presenting issue? |
| Reason for referral | Outside mandate / serious violence / child protection / sexual offense / domestic violence trigger / other. |
| Referral destination | Police/magistrates court / Probation and Social Welfare Officer/health or specialized support / other legally appropriate service. |
| Contact and date | Office/person contacted, date, phone/in-person/written mode. |
| Explanation to parties | What did the chairperson tell the parties about why referral is required and what happens next? |

| Record | Referral entered in register; written referral/order attached where law requires. |
| Feedback/follow-up | Date follow-up attempted; response received; next action; closure status. |
| Baseline reason for this addition
Formal coordination with JLOS actors is already widespread, but knowledge of where to refer and of how to explain referral, documentation, and feedback is weaker. The intervention should strengthen the referral loop, not merely increase contact. |
